What if the action an agent may not take had no path through the sampler?

A manifest of what may be done right now compiles into a token trie, and the trie becomes a mask on the sampler. At the step where the method name is chosen the model has three legal tokens out of 262,144. A capability outside the manifest is not improbable; it is absent. And because the enabled set is recomputed from world state, the surface can express what a JSON Schema structurally cannot — only after that. While a change is unrecorded, one opcode of forty-nine has a path, and it is the one that records it.

What we found, including the part that argues against us

The security advantage is smaller than the pitch. Measured on a local model across thirty turns per arm, a conventional tool-calling harness with live-state validation produced one forbidden call and caught it. Zero writes reached the closed record either way. Enforcement gives a different kind of guarantee — nothing to catch rather than everything caught — and that difference is load-bearing only when the validator is absent, incomplete, or bypassed.

The capability advantage is large. The same model completed none of ten legitimate tasks unmasked, and all ten masked, with fourteen malformed calls against none. The mechanism did not make a dangerous model safe. It made an unusable model usable — which matters exactly where data sovereignty forces you onto a small local model, and disappears against a frontier model that follows schemas reliably.

And it can cause a wrong write. Told to act on a record it may not touch, the model performs a legal action on a different record and reports success. Five times in twenty. The phrasing that triggers it is not an attack — both explicitly adversarial phrasings triggered it zero times out of twenty. What triggers it is a sentence that sounds like ordinary filing.

Independent work on frontier models found the same shape from the other direction. So the deployable form is propose-and-confirm, not autonomous writes, and we would rather say that here than have someone discover it in a clinic.

Why a clinical company publishes this

Because a claim about what software cannot do is worth nothing if the mechanism is not inspectable. "Our assistant cannot modify a signed record" is a sentence any vendor can write. Whether it is true depends on a mechanism, and a mechanism you cannot audit is a promise.

So the mechanism is open, two of the three demonstrations run without downloading a model — because what they show is a property of a compiled artefact, not a statistic over samples — and the failures are in the README rather than a footnote. What stays closed is the product, and anything that would map a live system's attack surface.

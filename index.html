capability-kernel Results

What if an agent could not emit the action it was not authorised to take?

Not rejected after the fact — unemittable. A manifest of what may be done right now compiles into a token trie, and the trie becomes a mask on the sampler. At the step where the method name is chosen the model has three legal tokens out of 262,144. A capability that is not in the manifest is not improbable; it is absent.

The measurements are the point, including the one that went against us: removing the illegal action turned a correct refusal into a legal write on the wrong record. That failure is triggered by requests that sound routine, not by attacks — and independent work on frontier models found the same shape.

Why a clinical company publishes this

Because the claim is only worth something if someone can check it. "Our assistant cannot modify a signed record" is a sentence any vendor can write. Whether it is true depends on a mechanism, and a mechanism you cannot inspect is a promise.

So the mechanism is open, the benchmarks run on a laptop, and the failures are in the README rather than in a footnote. What stays closed is the product and anything that would describe a live system's attack surface — the research is publishable, the deployment is not.
